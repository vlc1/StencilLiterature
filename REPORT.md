## 1. Architectural Positioning: Where `StencilAlgebra` Sits

Numerical PDE frameworks that target stencils or structured grids fall into five architectural families. `StencilAlgebra` occupies an uncrowded intersection: **a grid-free, rank-polymorphic, compile-time shift-sparse operator algebra in pure Julia** that derives the evaluation loop, the exact sparse Jacobian, and the matrix-free Jacobian-vector product (JVP) from one typed symbolic residual.

### Key Differentiators of `StencilAlgebra`

1. **Grid-size independence vs. `Symbolics.jl` / `MethodOfLines.jl` and `SparseConnectivityTracer.jl`:**
   Tracing an $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ array function constructs a symbolic graph or sparsity pattern of size $O(N_{\text{grid}})$ and requires graph coloring (`Gebremedhin2005Coloring`, `Hill2025SparseConnectivityTracer`) for every grid resolution. `StencilAlgebra` exploits translation invariance of the stencil support on $\mathbb{Z}^D$: symbolic canonicalization and differentiation cost $O(K)$ in the stencil tap count $K$, independent of grid size, and assemble `SparseMatrixCSC` or `SparseMatrixCSR` in a single straight-line pass without coloring.
2. **Discrete shift algebra vs. `UFL` / `Firedrake` and `Devito`:**
   Like `UFL` (`Alnaes2014UFL`), `StencilAlgebra` treats the residual $F(u)$ as a first-class algebraic term from which both the bilinear operator (`differentiate`) and directional derivative (`pushforward`) follow. Unlike `UFL` (continuous weak forms on finite elements) and `Devito`/`pystencils` (Python DSLs invoking an external C/CUDA compiler), `StencilAlgebra` embeds the shift algebra directly into Julia's type system (`AbstractField{T,O,S}`), relying on multiple dispatch and LLVM specialization to match hand-written loops within 10%.
3. **Dual-anchor shift-sparse representation (`InputAnchoring` vs. `OutputAnchoring`):**
   While the stencil literature writes output-anchored weights $J^k(\mathbf{p}) = \partial F(\mathbf{p})/\partial u(\mathbf{p} + \boldsymbol\alpha_k)$ (row/CSR affinity) and `SciPy`/LAPACK `DIA` formats align diagonals by column (CSC affinity), `StencilAlgebra` formalizes both as a $O(K)$ symbolic gauge transformation (`reanchor` via $\boldsymbol\delta_{-\boldsymbol\alpha_k}$ coefficient wraps, keeping keys first-axis-relative) and derives the exact unfolding comparator `AssemblyOrder{S,B}` so both CSC and CSR assemble without sorting or transposing.

## 2. Literature Synthesis on Open Design Challenges

### 2.1. Problem F1: Expanded JVP Cost, Tree-Level AD, and Equality Saturation

- **The issue in `StencilAlgebra` (`ROADMAP.md` §7, F1):**
  `pushforward(F, u, du)` currently computes $J v$ by contracting the canonical taps of `differentiate(F, u)`. Because canonical taps distribute shared factors across shifts (e.g., $\delta \Delta_h B$ becomes four separate multiplications by $-\delta$) and scalarize `SVector` operations into components, the Gray–Scott matrix-free product runs at $1.28\times$ a hand-written `SVector` loop ($465.8\;\mu\text{s}$ vs. $364.8\;\mu\text{s}$), whereas a hand-factored `StencilAlgebra` formula runs at $0.92\times$ ($335.4\;\mu\text{s}$).
- **What the literature does:**
  1. **Forward-mode AD on the expression DAG before tap expansion (`Alnaes2014UFL`, `Griewank2008EvaluatingDerivatives`):**
     In `UFL`, `action(derivative(F, u), du)` is computed by applying the forward chain rule directly on the expression tree ($d(a \cdot b) = da \cdot b + a \cdot db$, $d(u[\boldsymbol\alpha]) = du[\boldsymbol\alpha]$) rather than expanding into basis-element taps first and contracting second. Preserving the expression tree keeps factored diffusivity coefficients and `SVector` constructors intact, while `TappedField` expansion is reserved for `differentiate` (assembly and diagonal extraction).
  2. **Compile-time CSE and flop-reducing compiler passes (`Luporini2020Devito`, `Bauer2021Lbmpy`):**
     `Devito`'s Symbolic Engine (DSE) and `pystencils` separate *canonicalization* (used for alias/symmetry detection) from *loop-body optimization* (common subexpression elimination, factorization of shift-invariant weights, and SIMD vectorization across components).
  3. **E-graph equality saturation (`Willsey2021Egg`, `Cheli2021Metatheory`, `Yang2021Tensat`, `Gowda2022Symbolics`):**
     Classical term rewriting (`simplify.jl`) must orient distributivity in one direction (expanding products over taps to achieve a unique normal form). Equality saturation (`egg`, `Metatheory.jl`) inserts bidirectional rules—distributivity $a(b+c) \leftrightarrow ab + ac$, shift homomorphism $(a+b)[\boldsymbol\alpha] \leftrightarrow a[\boldsymbol\alpha] + b[\boldsymbol\alpha]$, and `SVector` component fusion—into an e-graph and extracts the minimum-cost expression. As shown in `Tensat` (`Yang2021Tensat`), when subexpressions are shared (DAG cost rather than tree cost), extraction via integer linear programming (ILP) or greedy CSE-aware heuristics prevents redundant evaluation while avoiding AC-associativity blow-up through bounded saturation depth.

### 2.2. Problem F2: Matrix-Free `pullback` ($J^\top \lambda$) and Boundary Semantics

- **The issue in `StencilAlgebra` (`PULLBACK.md`, `ROADMAP.md` §7, F2):**
  Algebraically, under `InputAnchoring`, the transposed product $(J^\top \lambda)(\mathbf{q}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol\alpha} A'^{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf{q})\, \lambda(\mathbf{q} - \boldsymbol\alpha)$ is a conflict-free gather. However, for a truncated operator $J_{RC}$ restricted to equation rows $R$ and unknown columns $C$, the term at column $\mathbf{q} \in C$ must only sum over shifts where $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{q} - \boldsymbol\alpha \in R$. Evaluating the field expression on boundary columns reads out-of-bounds indices or multiplies $0 \cdot \text{NaN}$ in ghost layers.
- **What the literature does:**
  1. **Scatter-to-gather index inversion with loop-bound peeling (`Huckelheim2019AdjointStencil`, `Li2018HalideAD`):**
     Hückelheim et al. (`Huckelheim2019AdjointStencil`) study this exact problem for reverse-mode AD of parallel stencil loops (combining `Tapenade` and `Devito`). Naive reverse-mode AD of a gather stencil loop produces a *scatter* loop over the forward interior domain $R$ (`u_bar[p + α] += ...`), which is bounds-safe (it only visits $\mathbf{p} \in R$) but requires atomic writes on GPUs. Transforming the adjoint into a thread-safe *gather* loop over $\mathbf{q} \in C$ requires either:
     - **Polyhedral loop peeling / point-split subdomains (`Li2018HalideAD`, `Huckelheim2019AdjointStencil`):** Partitioning $C$ into the interior core (where $\mathbf{q} - \boldsymbol\alpha \in R$ for all $\boldsymbol\alpha$) and boundary strips/boxes where only the in-range tap subsets are summed (`PULLBACK.md` Option D), or
     - **Guarded gather in the semantic evaluation loop (`PULLBACK.md` Option B):** Keeping the row-space membership test $[\mathbf{q} - \boldsymbol\alpha \in R]$ at the coordinate/lattice evaluation layer (`AssembledMatrix` / split-operator transpose), where line-segment bounds are already resolved without branching per inner point.
  2. **Halide's boundary-condition adjoint synthesis (`Li2018HalideAD`):**
     `Halide` synthesizes gather-based adjoint stencils by symbolically inverting read footprints and clamping/splitting the output domain into interior and boundary regions so that out-of-domain primal reads are structurally eliminated before GPU code generation.

### 2.3. Problems B2 & C2: GPU Sparse Formats (`CSR` vs. `DIA`/Stencil Storage)

- **The issue in `StencilAlgebra` (`GPU.md` §8, `ROADMAP.md` B2 & C2):**
  Choosing between assembling explicit `CuSparseMatrixCSR` / `ROCSparseMatrixCSR` matrices versus an in-house stencil storage format (one device array per non-constant tap) and matrix-free `pushforward`.
- **What the literature shows:**
  1. **Roofline and bandwidth trade-offs (`Kronbichler2019MatrixFree`, `Falgout2021HypreGPU`):**
     Stencils have low arithmetic intensity ($\sim 0.25\;\text{flop/byte}$ for a 2D 5-point Laplacian in Float64). As analyzed by Kronbichler & Kormann (`Kronbichler2019MatrixFree`) and confirmed in `GPU.md` §8, explicit CSR storage moves $1.4\text{--}1.9\times$ more bytes than offset-keyed stencil storage (`DIA`) due to column index traffic, and $\sim 3.5\times$ more bytes than matrix-free evaluation.
  2. **`hypre` `Struct`/`SStruct` on GPUs (`Falgout2021HypreGPU`):**
     Falgout et al. (`Falgout2021HypreGPU`) show that storing structured matrices as grids of stencil tap coefficients (`StructMatrix`, equivalent to `ROADMAP.md` C2's materialized order-2 lattice view) enables conflict-free device gathers for both $J v$ and $J^\top w$, trivial in-place coefficient refresh at each Newton step, and fused box-wise GPU kernels. Explicit CSR conversion is only needed when handing coarse-grid or separator problems to vendor solvers (`cuSPARSE`, `cuDSS`, `AmgX`, `rocSPARSE`).
  3. **Fine-grained parallel preconditioners on GPUs (`Chow2015FineGrainedILU`):**
     Standard triangular solves in ILU(0)/IC(0) suffer from sequential level-scheduling bottlenecks on GPUs; Chow & Patel (`Chow2015FineGrainedILU`) replace triangular solves with fine-grained Jacobi sweeps or polynomial/multigrid smoothers, reinforcing `StencilSolve.jl`'s focus on point-block Jacobi, Chebyshev, and structured multigrid/nested dissection.

### 2.4. Problems C3, C4 & C6: Splits, Pairing, Block Preconditioning, and GPU Direct/Multigrid Solvers

- **Operator preconditioning and causal pairing (`Mardal2011OperatorPreconditioning`, `Brown2012FieldSplit`, `PAIRING.md`):**
  `PAIRING.md` and `SPLIT-DERIVATIVES.md` adopt positional pairing of row and column splits (`PointSplit` and `ValueSplit`), leaving equation ordering to the modeller. This directly mirrors `PETSc`'s `PCFieldSplit` (`Brown2012FieldSplit`) and `hypre`'s `SStruct` numbering, while its mathematical justification for reaction–diffusion (diagonal Douglis–Nirenberg principal part) and Stokes saddle-point Schur complements follows Mardal & Winther (`Mardal2011OperatorPreconditioning`).
- **GPU Hierarchical Poincaré–Steklov / Cartesian Nested Dissection (`Melia2025JaxHPS`, `ROADMAP.md` C4):**
  Melia et al. (`Melia2025JaxHPS`) demonstrate in `jaxhps` that hierarchical domain decomposition on Cartesian boxes maps naturally to GPUs: leaf-box operators and planar separators have uniform sizes, turning sparse direct factorization into batched dense linear algebra (`cuSOLVER`/`BLAS`) with high arithmetic intensity.
- **Structured Coiteration for Splits and Masks (`Chou2018TACO`, `Ahrens2023Looplets`):**
  `StencilAlgebra`'s line-segment splitting for `Frame` ghost regions and compressed CSC/CSR fills (`src/materialize.jl`, `src/sparse.jl`) is a specialized, compile-time instance of `Finch.jl`'s "looplets" (`Ahrens2023Looplets`), which eliminate per-point branches by intersecting interval runs at loop boundaries.
